How accurately does Fiteo count calories? We tested it on 100 dishes.

Fiteo estimates calories and macros from a description of your meal — typed or spoken. Instead of claiming it works, we measured how far those estimates are from reference data. We publish the results in full, including where Fiteo gets it wrong.

Test of 21 October 2026 · 100 dishes, each 3 times · reference data: USDA FoodData Central and product labels

The test was run in Polish. The dish descriptions below are exactly what was typed into Fiteo, so they stay in Polish. Fiteo also understands meals described in English, but we haven’t repeated the accuracy test in English yet.

In short

  • When you give amounts (“150 g cooked rice, 120 g chicken breast”), Fiteo is off by 10 kcal per item on average. 94% of 85 dishes are within ±10%, and 100% within ±20%. Macros: on average 1.2 g protein, 0.8 g fat and 1.6 g carbs off.
  • The same description gives the same result. We sent each dish 3 times — the median spread between tries is 1% of the reference value.
  • Every single try, not averaged: 93% of all individual answers are within ±10%, and the median error of a single answer is 2%. We count this separately because errors up and down could cancel out in an average.
  • Branded products (Snickers, Danio, Prince Polo…): 100% within ±10%.
  • A description without amounts (“2 pancakes with cheese”) still means guessing the portion: a median error of 20%, on average +22%.

Where Fiteo gets it wrong

Descriptions without amounts. Only 33% of these dishes were within ±10%, and the spread between tries is larger (median 12%). The worst case: “6 pierogów ruskich” — three tries gave 390, 420, 990 kcal against a reference of 408 kcal. The takeaway: give the amount and accuracy improves several times over. Fiteo shows the assumed portion next to every item, so you can correct it straight away.

The biggest differences with a stated weight:

  • 125 g mozzarelli: reference 374 kcal, Fiteo 300 kcal (−20%)
  • 200 g ugotowanego makaronu: reference 316 kcal, Fiteo 268 kcal (−15%)
  • jabłko 180 g: reference 110 kcal, Fiteo 94 kcal (−14%)
  • 250 ml soku pomarańczowego: reference 127 kcal, Fiteo 112 kcal (−12%)

Some of these are more about the reference than about Fiteo: for example, the USDA value for mozzarella describes a different product from the typical ball of mozzarella sold in Poland. We didn’t adjust the set after the test — we publish it as it was fixed.

Describe meals with amounts and Fiteo counts almost as precisely as a label. Without amounts, it assumes a typical portion and shows it so you can correct it.

How we ran the test

  • The set was fixed before the test. We fixed the 100 dishes and their reference values before the test and didn’t change them after seeing the results.
  • Four groups: 35 products with a weight, 40 meals with an amount for each ingredient, 15 everyday descriptions without amounts and 10 branded products.
  • Reference data: USDA FoodData Central, Open Food Facts and product labels.
  • Just like in the app: each description went through Fiteo exactly as when you type it in the app. We tried every item 3 times — we show the middle value (median) and every try. Error = estimate − reference.
  • A whole day: we also checked how errors add up over a day of four meals.

Limitations

  • Nutrition tables are averages — a specific product in the shop can differ from them by a few to about fifteen percent.
  • Group C depends on the assumed portion, which is why we describe it for every dish.
  • The test measures estimates from a text description. We haven’t tested voice notes separately.

Fiteo is a tool for approximate calorie counting, not a medical device. Don’t use it to calculate medication doses, such as insulin. If you count calories for health reasons, talk to a doctor or dietitian.
