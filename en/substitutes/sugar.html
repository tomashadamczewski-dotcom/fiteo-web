Sugar substitute — what, how much and for what

Sugar has 400 kcal per 100 g and nothing else of nutritional value. There are plenty of substitutes, but they differ in sweetness, calories and how they behave in baking.

How much to use instead of sugar

  • Erythritol — about 70% as sweet as sugar: instead of 100 g sugar, use about 130 g erythritol. Almost no calories and doesn’t raise blood sugar. In larger amounts it can feel cooling in the mouth.
  • Xylitol — as sweet as sugar, so 1:1, but with about 40% fewer calories. It is highly toxic to dogs — keep it away from pets.
  • Stevia — hundreds of times sweeter than sugar, used by the pinch or drop. Good for drinks; in baking it adds no volume.
  • Fruit (banana, dates, applesauce) — in cakes and oatmeal it adds sweetness and moisture, but it has calories and natural sugars.
  • Honey and syrups — they’re still sugar. They’re slightly sweeter, so you can use a little less, but you save few calories.

Polyols in large amounts can have a laxative effect — start with small portions.

FAQ

Which sugar substitute is best for baking?

Erythritol and xylitol, because they give volume similar to sugar. Stevia sweetens, but it can’t replace sugar in the structure of a cake.

Is honey healthier than sugar?

Calorie-wise there’s little difference — about 300 kcal per 100 g. If calories are what matters, erythritol or stevia are the better choice.

How much erythritol instead of a spoon of sugar?

About 1.3 spoons of erythritol, because it’s less sweet than sugar.
