How we calculate

  • Basal metabolic rate (BMR) — the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: 10 × weight [kg] + 6.25 × height [cm] − 5 × age + 5 (men) or − 161 (women).
  • Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) — BMR × an activity factor: 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.6 / 1.75.
  • Target — to lose weight we subtract a deficit, to gain we add a surplus. 1 kg of body tissue is roughly 7,700 kcal, so 0.5 kg a week is about 550 kcal a day.
  • Macros — 30% of energy from protein, 30% from fat, 40% from carbs.

How much to eat to gain muscle

Choose the “Gain” goal. The calculator adds a surplus to your needs: 0.25 kg a week is about 275 kcal a day, 0.5 kg about 550 kcal. For most people 0.25–0.5 kg a week is sensible; faster gains are usually more fat than muscle.

  • Muscle grows from training, not from calories alone. A surplus without strength training is mostly fat.
  • Protein: about 1.6–2.2 g per kilogram of body weight a day is the usual advice — the calculator’s 30/30/40 split is a starting point.
  • Check every 2–3 weeks: if your weight doesn’t move, add about 100–200 kcal; if it rises faster than 0.5 kg a week, take some away.

You know your target. Now the hard part: counting

The calculator tells you how much to eat. In practice it’s harder to know how much you ate. In Fiteo you log a meal by typing or recording a voice note — recipes from reels are saved with their calories straight away, and you can swap any ingredient: for something lighter, higher in protein, lactose-free, vegetarian, cheaper or described in your own words.

Accuracy depends on the description. When you give amounts (“150 g rice, 120 g chicken breast”), the median error of Fiteo’s estimate against reference data is about 1%. Without amounts (“a plate of spaghetti”) Fiteo assumes a typical portion and shows it next to the item so you can correct it — then the median error grows to about 20%. The more precisely you describe a meal, the more accurate the result. We tested it on 100 dishes described in Polish: see the accuracy test results.

FAQ

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) minus a deficit. A deficit of about 550 kcal a day means roughly 0.5 kg less a week. The calculator above works it out for your details.

What pace of weight loss is safe?

The usual advice is no more than about 1% of body weight a week. The calculator won’t let you pick a faster pace and won’t show a target below the safe minimum.

How many calories should I eat to gain muscle?

A surplus of about 250–500 kcal a day above your total needs is usually enough — roughly 0.25–0.5 kg a week. A bigger surplus more often means more fat than muscle. Muscle only grows with strength training and enough protein (usually about 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight).

What’s the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the energy your body needs at rest. TDEE is your total daily need — BMR multiplied by an activity factor.

Is the calculator accurate?

It’s an estimate from a formula that is off by a few to about fifteen percent for most people. Treat it as a starting point and adjust after 2–3 weeks based on your weight.

Why shouldn’t I eat below my BMR?

Very low energy intake makes it hard to get enough protein and nutrients, and it’s hard to sustain. For large deficits, talk to a doctor or dietitian.

The calculator gives an approximate result and does not replace advice from a doctor or dietitian. It is not intended for pregnant or breastfeeding women, children or people with conditions that require a special diet.
